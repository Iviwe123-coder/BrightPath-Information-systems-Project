Data Ethics, Privacy and POPIA

Topic 2 · student and staff personal information in South African higher education

BrightPath is a fictional South African higher education institution. Its student, staff, learning and finance records can contain personal information, from contact details and marks to payment and support records. POPIA gives the institution a framework for handling that information lawfully and responsibly; the practical controls should follow the data through collection, use, sharing, storage and deletion.

Lawful processing and POPIA

POPIA sets eight conditions for lawful processing, including accountability, purpose, information quality, security and data subject participation (sections 4 and 8–25). Consent is one possible justification, not the only one; section 11 also lists other grounds. BrightPath should document each process’s purpose and justification, and collect only adequate, relevant and necessary information. (POPIA)

Purpose, access and retention

BrightPath should explain why it collects information and who may receive it. Reuse must be compatible with the original purpose or meet an applicable basis in POPIA (sections 13–15). Keep records only as long as needed or legally required, then securely destroy or de-identify them. Give staff access according to their role.

Data subject participation

Students and staff may ask whether BrightPath holds their information and request access, correction or deletion in the circumstances set out in POPIA (sections 23–24). Provide a clear request route and update connected systems when a correction is accepted. (POPIA)

Students under 18 and sensitive information

Some students may be under 18. POPIA generally prohibits processing children’s information unless an exception or Regulator authorisation applies; examples include consent from a competent person or necessity to establish a legal right (sections 34–35). Special personal information, such as health details, has separate rules (section 26). Check the specific facts before processing. (POPIA)

Third-party and cloud-hosted systems

If a cloud provider hosts BrightPath’s LMS, document each party’s role, approved access, security, retention and deletion. POPIA requires an operator to act with the responsible party’s authorisation and be bound by a written security agreement (section 21). Cross-border transfers must meet a condition in section 72; cloud hosting is not automatically allowed or prohibited. (POPIA)

Security safeguards and breach response

BrightPath must use reasonable technical and organisational safeguards (section 19). If there are reasonable grounds to believe information was accessed by an unauthorised person, the responsible party must notify the Regulator and affected people as soon as reasonably possible; an operator must alert the responsible party (section 22). Escalate suspected incidents promptly and follow current Regulator guidance. (POPIA) (Information Regulator)

Ethical use of student data and analytics

A low LMS activity signal may prompt a supportive check-in, but it does not prove disengagement; a student may study offline or have limited connectivity. Explain indicators, let people correct errors, restrict access and use human review. POPIA also limits certain solely automated decisions with legal or substantial effects (section 71). (POPIA)
