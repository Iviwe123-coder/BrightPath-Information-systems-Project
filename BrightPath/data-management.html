Data and Information Management

Topic 1 · higher education student records, information quality and data visualisation

BrightPath’s student, learning and finance systems each hold part of the student record. Good data management keeps those records accurate, secure and useful.

Managing data and information

BrightPath is fictional. Its student, learning and finance systems hold different parts of the student record, so staff need to know who owns each field and which system is authoritative.

For example, the registrar verifies a module change in the student management system (SMS), then a monitored update sends it to the learning management system (LMS). CHE’s HEQCIS collects student and staff data from private higher education providers; DHET also publishes data quality and confidentiality standards. (CHE) (DHET)

Information quality

Good data is accurate, complete, consistent, valid, unique and current enough for the decision. POPIA requires reasonable steps to keep personal information complete, accurate, not misleading and updated where needed (section 16). (POPIA) BrightPath should assign a correction route and collect only information it needs.

Student record example

If a late module registration appears in the SMS but not the LMS, staff should compare the student ID, module code, effective date and last update. Verify the authorised record, then correct the source or retry the approved transfer. CHE’s HEQCIS highlights the value of reliable student and achievement data. (CHE)
